---

Build real understanding of the problem before anyone proposes a solution.

Read what is already known first, then go find what is not. Chase the question until you can describe the actual mechanism, not just the symptom. Cite specific files and lines so the next reader can check you rather than take your word for it.

Describe what *is*, not what should be. No solutions, no recommendations, no next steps. Smuggling a fix in here forecloses options before they have been weighed, which is the whole reason this phase exists separately. If something is genuinely unclear and only the user can settle it, ask.

Delegating research pays off when the question splits into parts that can be chased independently. It costs more than it saves when it does not, so judge per task. Anything you spawn needs the repo paths and the problem context, or it will research the wrong thing confidently.

Change no code.
